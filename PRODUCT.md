# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Mark and a small allow-list of people who make briefs. Anyone with a share link can read a shared brief or watch a shared collection without an account.

Briefs are made and read mostly at a desk, with real use on a phone. Shared collections are sent to one person at a time: because they asked a question a collection already answers, or because a conversation led Mark to build one for them. The recipient opens the link once, usually at a desk, sometimes on a phone, and has never seen Brief before. Mark watches a collection himself while assembling it, on desktop, and rarely revisits it after.

## Product Purpose

Brief turns a YouTube video into a structured, timestamped brief and keeps every brief in a searchable personal library. It answers whether a video deserves attention before anyone pays for it with forty minutes, and the library then works as memory: half-remembered ideas found again by searching a fragment. See `docs/CONTEXT.md` for the full intent.

Collections are an ordered set of clips Mark assembles by hand to show someone why he holds a view, through the videos that formed it. Each entry carries a short note under it (generated, then editable), and that stays. Beyond that, a collection is the videos and their order: new kinds of curator-written text, such as an introduction for the whole collection, may come later and are out of scope until then.

Success: a brief answers "worth it?" at a glance, a search lands fast enough that nobody reaches for Google instead, and a collection recipient watches it through and understands the point.

## Brand Personality

Calm, sharp, warm. Quiet confidence that gets to the point and reads as made by a person rather than a company. Brief pages put triage first and then read well for anyone who stays. Shared collections sit slightly toward the crafted end: they should feel put together with care for the person receiving them, while the clips stay the main event.

## Anti-references

- A YouTube clone: red-and-white video-site chrome, thumbnail walls, recommendation-feed energy.
- Generic SaaS: cream backgrounds, gradient buttons, hero metrics, identical card grids.
- A dense admin panel: gray tables, tiny type, everything boxed in.
- AI-product gloss: purple gradients, sparkle icons, "powered by AI" announcements.

## Design Principles

1. **Answer "worth it?" first.** Every surface leads with what helps someone decide where their attention goes, then rewards the reader who stays.
2. **The videos are the main event.** On collections especially, design frames the clips and the order they come in. It never competes with them.
3. **Care shows as precision, not decoration.** Warmth comes from typography, spacing and restraint, not ornaments or effects.
4. **Fast, or it is just storage.** Search and navigation have to feel instant; a slow path sends people elsewhere.
5. **Every screen owes the basics.** Phone, desktop, dark mode, keyboard and screen readers all work properly, whether or not they serve triage.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: readable contrast in both themes, full keyboard operation, meaningful labels for screen readers, and reduced motion honored.
